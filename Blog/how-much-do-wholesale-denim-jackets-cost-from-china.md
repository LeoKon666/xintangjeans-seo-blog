## Denim Jacket Wholesale Price Components

A $18 FOB jacket can balloon to $32 landed cost when you overlook these five elements. What we've seen repeatedly is buyers focusing only on the unit price while ignoring the add-ons that actually determine profitability.

### 1. Base Fabric Cost (Per Yard)
Low-grade (8-10oz) runs $1.80-$2.50 but tends to shrink badly—expect 150+ washes if you're lucky. Mid-grade (12-14oz) at $3.20-$4.80 is Xintang's bestseller for standard retail quality. Premium (15oz+) starts at $6.50-$9.00 when you're sourcing selvedge or Japanese/Korean mill fabrics.

Basic trucker jackets add $2.50-$3.80 in labor. Cargo or utility styles with multiple pockets? Tack on another $4.20-$6.50. Distressed or embellished designs require hand-processing, which means +$8.00-$12.00 per piece.

## Step-by-Step: Calculating True Landed Cost

### Step 1: Verify Fabric Specs
Always request GSM (grams per square meter) tests. That "14oz" claim could actually be 12oz, saving the supplier $1.20/yard while quietly downgrading your product.

### Step 2: Decode Washing Fees
Standard enzyme wash costs $0.80-$1.20/jacket. Stone wash runs $1.50-$2.80. Laser distressing? Budget $3.50-$7.00. The factories that stand out are usually transparent about these charges upfront, not at the pre-shipment stage.

### Step 3: Account for Trims
YKK zippers add $1.20 versus generic at $0.35. Yes, your customer will notice the difference. Topstitching thread quality varies by $0.15-$0.40/unit depending on durability requirements.

### Step 4: Shipping & Duties
Air freight (7-10 days) costs $6.50-$8.50/kg. Sea LCL (30-45 days) drops that to $1.20-$2.00/kg. US and EU duties run 8-12% of FOB value under denim HS code 6203.42. Factor these in from day one, not when the invoice arrives.

## 3 Costly Mistakes Buyers Make

**Ignoring shrinkage rates** is the number one issue we see. Low-grade denim can shrink 8-12% after first wash. Order 15% extra fabric to compensate, or deal with returns from angry customers who suddenly own crop jackets.

**Underestimating QC costs** is another trap. For orders exceeding $5,000, budget $0.30-$0.80/unit for third-party inspection. Skipping this step because "the factory seems trustworthy" has burned more brands than we can count.

**Missing payment terms leverage** leaves money on the table. Negotiating 30% deposit plus 70% balance gets you 8-12% better pricing than paying 100% upfront. Suppliers respect buyers who understand cash flow dynamics.
