Most Chinese jeans manufacturers set high MOQs - typically 300-500 pairs per style - and they have their reasons. Production efficiency matters when you're running machines optimized for identical items in large batches. Profit margins get squeezed on smaller orders with higher per-unit costs. And shipping economics demand volume when you're filling containers.

But Xintang is different.

## Step-by-Step: How To Place Small Wholesale Orders

### Step 1: Identify True Low-MOQ Suppliers

Start with Alibaba, but add the filter for verified suppliers and search "no MOQ jeans" - you'll get hundreds of results, most of them garbage. The factories that stand out are usually Xintang-based (that's where 85% of China's denim production happens). Don't commit to anyone before placing a sample order. Test first.

### Step 2: Negotiate Order Terms

Bundle multiple styles to meet minimums if you're falling short. Offer faster payment terms in exchange for flexibility - factories love cash flow predictability. Request "shared production" slots where your order runs alongside similar orders from other buyers.

### Step 3: Optimize Shipping Costs

For orders under 200 pairs, air freight makes sense despite the higher cost per unit. Above 200 pairs, sea shipping wins. Some buyers consolidate with other small orders through freight forwarders, splitting container space and costs.

### Step 4: Quality Control

Insist on pre-shipment photos - not the glamour shots, the real warehouse photos showing your actual inventory. Require third-party inspections for first orders (yes, even small ones). Start with smaller test orders before scaling up. Most quality disasters happen because someone skipped this step to save $200.

## 5 Mistakes To Avoid With Small Orders

**Choosing the wrong supplier type** - Trading companies look like factories online but they're middlemen with higher MOQs.

**Neglecting quality control** - Small orders still need inspection. A bad batch of 100 pairs hurts just as much as 1,000 when you're operating on thin margins.

**Over-customizing first orders** - Simple designs have lower MOQs because suppliers can slot them into existing production schedules. Your custom-everything vision can wait until order two.

**Poor timing** - Avoid placing your first order anywhere near Chinese New Year (January-February) when factories shut down for weeks and rush orders before the holiday compress quality control timelines.

**Underestimating shipping costs** - Air freight can double your landed cost, turning a $12 jean into a $16.50 jean before it hits your shelf. Run the numbers before you commit.

## FAQ: Small Quantity Jeans Orders

**Q: What's the smallest order you can place?**  
A: Some Xintang suppliers accept orders as small as 20-50 pairs for sample testing. To be fair, you'll get better pricing at 100+ pairs, but the option exists.

**Q: How much more expensive are small orders?**  
A: Expect to pay 20-30% more per pair compared to 500+ piece orders. Why does this matter? Because proper supplier selection can cut that premium in half - the wrong supplier charges 50% more and makes you feel lucky to get it.

**Q: Can I get custom labels on small orders?**  
A: Yes, most suppliers offer private labeling starting at 150 pairs, though setup fees will be higher than on large runs. Budget $200-300 for label production.

**Q: What's the best payment method for small orders?**  
A: PayPal or credit card offers buyer protection for initial orders under $2,000, which matters when you're working with a new supplier. Larger orders typically use wire transfer - less protection but lower fees.

**Q: How long do small orders take to produce?**
