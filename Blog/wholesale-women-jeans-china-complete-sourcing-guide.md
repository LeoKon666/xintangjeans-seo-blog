## Women's Jeans Styles: What's Selling in 2026

### High-Waisted Wide Leg
This style works for curvy fits and vintage-inspired boutiques. You want an 11-12" front rise with a 22-24" leg opening. When you place the order, specify the stretch percentage—2-3% elastane is the sweet spot. Too much stretch and they bag out after one wear.

### Y2K Low-Rise Flare
Youth-focused online stores can't keep these in stock. The specs matter: 7-8" front rise, 20-22" at the knee opening up to 24-26" at the hem. Request pre-shrunk denim upfront or you'll deal with returns from customers who washed them once.

### Cargo Denim
Streetwear retailers keep reordering these. Look for 10oz+ fabric weight and reinforced pocket stitching—the pockets are the whole point. Metal hardware upgrades run an extra $0.50-1.50 per pair but make a visible difference.

### Distressed Mom Jeans
These are the bread and butter for general fashion boutiques. Medium blue wash with knee-level distressing. Order an extra 5% beyond what you think you need because size exchanges are inevitable.

## Step-by-Step: How to Place Your First Wholesale Order

### Step 1: Request Swatches First
Always get physical fabric samples before ordering. Screen photos lie about color and texture. Reputable suppliers like Xintang Jeans provide swatch books showing 15-20 denim weights and washes—if they won't send swatches, that's your first red flag.

### Step 2: Verify Sizing Specs
Women's sizing varies wildly by market, more than you'd think. Either provide your own size chart or request the factory's measurement table in centimeter increments. This step alone prevents most of the "these don't fit" complaints.

### Step 3: Order Prototypes
Pay the $50-100 sample fee for 1-2 prototypes in your target sizes. What are you checking? Stitch density should hit 8-12 stitches per inch, pocket placement needs to be consistent, and zipper quality matters more than people realize—cheap zippers break and customers remember.

### Step 4: Finalize Wash Details
For distressed styles, you need to approve exact bleach levels, sanding locations, and thread color for repairs. "Distressed" means different things to different factories. Get specific or you'll get whatever they felt like doing that day.

### Step 5: Arrange Inspection
Third-party inspection costs $150-300. It catches 90% of quality issues before shipping. Skip this step and you're gambling with thousands of dollars.

## 5 Costly Mistakes First-Time Buyers Make

**Assuming "Asian sizing" fits Western customers**  
Most factories use Chinese size charts by default. A US size 6 translates to Chinese XL. Yes, really. This isn't a theory—we've seen multiple buyers get stuck with inventory they can't sell because they skipped the sizing conversation.

**Skipping pre-shipment inspection**  
Without inspection, expect 5-15% defects in bulk orders. That's not a worst-case scenario. That's typical. Some buyers learn this lesson once and never skip inspection again. Don't be the one who has to learn it the hard way.

**Not accounting for shrinkage**  
Unsanforized denim shrinks 3-5%. Always ask if washes are pre-shrunk. If they're not, your "perfect fit" jeans become highwaters after the first wash.

**Overlooking payment terms**  
Standard is 30% deposit, 70% before shipment. Never pay 100% upfront unless you enjoy giving away money to strangers. Factories that demand full payment upfront usually have cash flow problems—their problem shouldn't become yours.

**Choosing price over reliability**  
The cheapest supplier often becomes the most expensive after you factor in reworks, delays, and replacement orders. A supplier charging $2 more per pair who actually delivers on time is cheaper than the "bargain" supplier who ships three weeks late with quality issues.
