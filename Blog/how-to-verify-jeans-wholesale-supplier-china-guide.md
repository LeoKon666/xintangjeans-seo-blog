## Step-by-Step Supplier Verification Process

### Step 1: Check Business Registration Documents

Every legitimate Chinese manufacturer has an official business license (营业执照), export license (if shipping internationally), and tax registration certificate.

**How to verify:**
1. Request clear scans (not photos) of all documents
2. Check registration number at [China's National Enterprise Credit System](http://www.gsxt.gov.cn)
3. Confirm address matches factory location

Blurry documents or refusal to share licenses? Walk away.

### Step 2: Verify Physical Factory Existence

For most brands, this step separates real factories from trading companies posing as manufacturers. We use live video tours via WhatsApp or WeChat (not pre-recorded footage), Google Earth view of factory address, third-party inspection reports like SGS, and request production floor photos with today's newspaper visible in the frame.

![fabric quality control inspection textile](unsplash:fabric+quality+control+inspection+textile)

### Step 3: Assess Production Capacity

Ask specific questions: How many sewing lines do you operate? What's your monthly jeans production capacity? Can you show me your cutting room and washing facilities?

**Typical Xintang factory capacities:**
| Factory Size | Monthly Output | MOQ |
|--------------|----------------|-----|
| Small workshop | 5,000-10,000 pairs | 100-300 pairs |
| Medium factory | 30,000-50,000 pairs | 500-1,000 pairs |
| Large facility | 100,000+ pairs | 3,000+ pairs |

Why does this matter? Because a factory claiming 100,000 pair monthly capacity but offering 50-pair MOQs is lying about something.

### Step 4: Evaluate Quality Control Processes

Reliable suppliers will have a dedicated QC team (ask for staff ratio), pre-shipment inspection reports, standard measurement charts with plus/minus tolerance specified, and a documented sample approval process.

Request their defect rate percentage. Good factories maintain under 3%. The ones claiming zero defects are probably lying - every factory has some margin of error, and honest suppliers admit it.

### Step 5: Test Communication Responsiveness

We track average response time to emails (under 24 hours is good), willingness to answer technical questions, availability during your business hours, and English proficiency level. To be fair, not every great factory has perfect English, but they should at least try to answer your questions directly instead of giving vague responses.

### Step 6: Check References & Online Reputation

Look at Alibaba transaction history (real orders, not just ratings), industry forums like Fashion-Incubator, Google search "[supplier name] scam", and ask for 2-3 reference clients you can contact. Yes, the references might be cherry-picked, but a supplier who refuses to provide any references is hiding something.

### Step 7: Negotiate Safe Payment Terms

**Recommended payment structure:**
1. 30% deposit after sample approval
2. 70% balance after pre-shipment inspection
3. Never wire 100% upfront

Use Alibaba Trade Assurance or PayPal for buyer protection as an alternative. The factories that stand out are usually the ones flexible enough to work with your comfort level on payments, especially for first orders.

## Common Wholesale Jeans Scams to Avoid

**Bait-and-Switch Quality**  
Samples come from one factory, bulk order ships from another. This is the most common scam we see.

**Fake Certifications**  
Forged ISO or OEKO-TEX certificates that look legitimate but don't check out when you verify the registration numbers.

**Hidden Fees**  
Unexpected charges for "export packaging" or "customs clearance" that weren't in the original quote.

**Ghost Factories**  
Trading companies posing as manufacturers. They take your deposit, place the order with an actual factory, pocket the difference, and disappear when quality issues arise.

**Sample Scams**  
Perfect samples but bulk quality is completely different - sometimes intentionally, sometimes because they outsourced production to a cheaper facility.
