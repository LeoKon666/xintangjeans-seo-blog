## The 5-Point Wholesale Jeans Inspection System

### Step 1: Fabric Quality Verification

Start with these hands-on tests.

**Fabric Weight Check**
Use a GSM cutter (available for $15 on AliExpress). Cut a 10cm x 10cm square from a hidden area like the pocket bag. Weigh on a precision scale with 0.01g accuracy, then compare to supplier's claimed GSM (grams per square meter).

For 12oz denim, expect 400-420 GSM. Below 380GSM indicates lightweight, less durable fabric. That's the whole point of this test.

**Composition Test**
Pull a few threads from the seam allowance for a burn test. 100% cotton burns cleanly to ash with a paper-like smell, while polyester blends melt into hard beads with a chemical odor.

**Stretch Recovery**
Stretch fabric 2" sideways for 10 seconds. Quality denim should return to within 0.5" of original size—poor recovery indicates cheap elastane blends that won't hold up.

![denim fabric quality control inspection textile](unsplash:denim+fabric+quality+control+inspection+textile)

### Step 2: Stitch Work Analysis

Check these critical stitching points.

The back yoke seam should have double parallel stitching. Single stitch here causes early failure—we've seen it happen too many times. Look for bartack reinforcements at pocket corners; loose threads indicate poor tension control during production. Quality jeans use 5-7 stitches per inch for hemming, so count stitches in a 1" section to verify. Finally, check seam allowance for a minimum 0.5" raw edge allowance, as narrow allowances fray faster.

Premium manufacturers like Xintang Jeans use 16-18 stitches per inch on stress points versus 12-14 in budget production. Why does this matter? Because those extra stitches are what separate jeans that last two years from ones that fail in six months.

### Step 3: Wash & Dye Consistency

Follow this wash evaluation protocol.

**Batch Comparison**
Select 3 random pairs from different packing boxes. Lay flat under natural light—color variance should be ≤5% (use Pantone swatches for accuracy).

**Fade Test**
Rub denim vigorously with white cloth. Minimal indigo transfer indicates good dye fixation, while heavy bleeding predicts premature fading after customer use.

**Wash Effect Uniformity**
Check distressed areas match sample pattern. Hand-sanded areas should show gradual wear, not harsh lines. Laser fading should have soft edges, not crisp boundaries that look artificial.

### Step 4: Hardware & Trims Assessment

Test these components systematically:

| Component | Test Method | Pass Standard |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Zippers | 50 open/close cycles | Smooth operation, no teeth skipping |
| Buttons | 10lb pull test | No detachment or cracking |
| Rivets | Pry with flat tool | No lifting from fabric |
| Labels | Rub test | No ink smudging after friction |

### Step 5: Sizing Compliance Check

Measure against spec sheet for waistband (both stretched and relaxed measurements), inseam (from crotch to hem), leg opening (lay flat, measure across), and front rise (waistband to crotch seam).

Accept ±0.5" variance on key measurements. Beyond this indicates poor pattern control—and that's a red flag for the entire production batch.

## Common Inspection Mistakes (And How Xintang Jeans Avoids Them)

**Mistake #1: Only Checking Sample Units**
Factories often hand-make samples then bulk-produce differently. To be fair, this isn't always intentional deception—sometimes it's just the reality of scaling production. Our solution? Xintang provides pre-shipment bulk inspection reports that verify actual production quality.

**Mistake #2: Ignoring Wash Care Labels**
Incorrect care instructions cause customer returns, plain and simple. We use ISO-certified lab testing for all care labels to prevent this costly mistake.

**Mistake #3: Not Testing Across Size Run**
Sizing issues often appear at extremes (XS/XXL). The factories that stand out are usually the ones that include size-run validation in their QC protocol.

**Mistake #4: Rushing Inspection**
Critical flaws hide in seams and linings. That's why we enforce a 48-hour minimum inspection window—no exceptions.
